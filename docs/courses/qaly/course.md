# The QALY: what it is, how it is made, and why people fight about it
slug: qaly
level: Introductory graduate level (health economics / health policy students); terms defined when first used
language: English
notation: Health-related quality of life weight (utility) q, 1 = full health, 0 = dead, below 0 = worse than dead; time t in years; QALYs = sum of q × t (area under the quality curve); ΔQ = incremental QALYs, ΔC = incremental cost; ICER = ΔC / ΔQ; threshold λ (cost per QALY a payer accepts); discount rate r
example: A 60-year-old with a chronic disease. Standard care: 4 years at q = 0.5 (2.0 QALYs). New treatment: 5 years at q = 0.7 (3.5 QALYs), costing 30,000 more. Gain ΔQ = 1.5 QALYs; ICER = 20,000 per QALY. Returned to in every lecture.

Five short lectures on the quality-adjusted life year: what it measures, how the numbers are produced, the utility theory it rests on, and the ethical and empirical objections — and what can be said for it anyway.
