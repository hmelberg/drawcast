QUESTION: How much do rich countries spend on health, and does spending more buy longer lives?

NAIVE ANSWER: The biggest spender should live longest. If America spends the most, Americans should live longest.

INSIGHT: Health money buys years quickly at first and then barely at all. Past a few thousand dollars a head, the length of a life depends on what the money buys and how people live, not on how much is spent.

EXAMPLE: OECD figures for about 2022, rounded. These are spending per person per year in US dollars adjusted for purchasing power, with life expectancy at birth.
- Mexico: about $1,200, about 75 years.
- Spain: about $4,400, about 83 years.
- UK: about $5,500, about 81 years.
- Japan: about $5,000, about 84 years.
- Germany: about $8,000, about 81 years.
- Switzerland: about $8,000, about 84 years.
- United States: about $12,500, about 77 years.
- OECD average spending: about $5,000.

The ghost point is a reading of the curve, not data. Where the curve's level reaches $12,500, it suggests at least 84 years. The seven-year gap is between that ghost and the real point.
