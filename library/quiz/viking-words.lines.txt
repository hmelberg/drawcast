Window is Norse vindauga: a wind-eye, the hole in the roof that let the smoke out and the air in.
Husband is húsbóndi, the one who keeps the house.
Sky, egg and knife came the same way, from Vikings who settled in northern and eastern England from the eight hundreds.
For centuries, the old English word for eggs lived on beside it: eyren.
In 1490 the printer William Caxton told of a merchant who asked a woman in Kent for eggs, and was told she spoke no French.
She only understood when someone else asked for eyren.
Beef and chair are French: they came with the Normans after 1066, and table, older, was reinforced by them.
And one of the commonest words in English is Norse as well: they.
