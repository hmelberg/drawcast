The champion is the elephant: about twenty-two months, almost two years.
A giraffe takes about fifteen months, and its calf arrives with a drop of about one and a half metres to the ground.
We come third, with nine months.
Then a big drop: a dog needs only about two months.
And a golden hamster just sixteen days, one of the shortest of any mammal.
So why does the elephant wait so long? Its calf is born big, at about a hundred kilograms.
And it is born ready: within hours it stands, and soon walks with the herd.
On the open savanna, a baby that cannot keep up does not survive. So the long wait pays.
