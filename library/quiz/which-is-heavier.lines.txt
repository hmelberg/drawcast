A blue whale's heart is the biggest heart on Earth, yet the one in a Toronto museum weighs about a hundred and eighty kilos. A small car weighs five times as much.
But the air in a bedroom outweighs a ten-year-old. Each cubic metre weighs over a kilo, and a bedroom holds about forty.
And a puffy summer cloud, a kilometre across, holds about five hundred tonnes of water: more than a fully loaded jumbo jet.
So why doesn't it fall? Its droplets are so tiny that rising warm air holds them up.
Weight hides in things spread thin: air and mist feel like nothing, but there is a lot of them.
Next time a cloud drifts by, picture a jumbo jet's worth of water floating over your head.
