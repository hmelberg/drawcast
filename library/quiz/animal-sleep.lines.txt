The champion is the brown bat, asleep about twenty hours a day, and awake mainly at dusk, when the insects fly.
A cat manages twelve hours or more: a hunter can eat in a few big meals, and sleep where little hunts it.
Giraffes in zoos sleep under five hours, in short naps, often standing up.
And wild African elephants sleep about two hours a day, the least of any mammal measured.
Researchers in Botswana put sleep trackers on two elephant matriarchs.
Some nights, they went forty-six hours without any sleep at all.
Big plant-eaters spend most of their day eating, and sleep little; a small animal in a safe roost can sleep the day away.
