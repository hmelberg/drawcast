It opened in 1889, for the World's Fair, a hundred years after the French Revolution began.
At three hundred metres, it was the tallest structure on Earth,
until New York's Chrysler Building passed it in 1930.
But Paris did not love it at first. In 1887, artists and writers signed a letter calling it useless and monstrous.
One of them, the story goes, was Guy de Maupassant, who later ate lunch in the tower, because it was the one place in Paris he could not see it.
And it was never meant to last. Eiffel's permit ran for just twenty years, until 1909.
So Eiffel gave his tower a job: weather instruments, experiments, and above all radio antennas at the top.
By 1909 it was the army's radio station: too useful to pull down.
In the First World War it caught enemy radio messages. The tower that was meant to come down is still standing.
