(a) Here's a famous puzzle about a woman called Linda. Which story about her is more probable?
(a) Linda is thirty-one, single, outspoken and very bright.
(a) She studied philosophy. As a student she cared deeply about discrimination, and marched against nuclear weapons.

(a) Yet in Tversky and Kahneman's 1983 study, about eighty-five percent of people picked the second answer.

(a) Exactly. They called it the conjunction fallacy: each added detail makes a story less likely, but more believable.
