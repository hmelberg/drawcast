Africa is about fourteen times bigger: thirty million square kilometres against two.
The culprit is the map itself, drawn by Gerardus Mercator in 1569 for sailors.
It keeps compass directions true, so a straight line on it is a steady course across the sea.
But to do that it stretches the land more and more toward the poles, and Greenland sits far in the north.
In fact Greenland is smaller than a single African country, the Democratic Republic of the Congo.
And Africa could swallow the United States, China and India together, with room to spare.
So next time you see a world map, look twice at the top and bottom.
