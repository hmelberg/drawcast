Twist a Rubik's cube a few times, and it's a mess. But how many different messes are there?
Each step along this line is ten times the one before.
[ASK] How many different positions can a Rubik's cube be in? Click on the line.
  [right] Close. It is about forty-three quintillion.
  [wrong] Most people guess far too low.
Forty-three quintillion: a forty-three followed by eighteen zeros.
Show one new position every second, and you'd need about a hundred times the age of the universe to see them all.
And yet every single one can be solved in twenty moves or fewer.
That limit is called God's number. A team proved it in 2010, using about thirty-five years of computer time donated by Google.
They did not solve the positions one by one: they grouped them into huge families, and used the cube's symmetry to deal with whole families at once.
So no mess is ever more than twenty twists from home, even if finding those twenty is the hard part.
