A right-angled triangle, with sides a, b and c: three, four and five. How are the three sides linked?
Build a square on each side, and the answer is hiding in their areas.
[ASK] Which tile finishes the theorem, so that it holds for every right triangle?
  [right] c squared: the two small squares together fill the big one.
  [wrong] Look at what the squares measure: areas.
Check it here. The square on the short side holds nine little squares,
the one on the side of four holds sixteen,
and nine plus sixteen is twenty-five: exactly the big square.
It carries the name of Pythagoras, a Greek of about 500 BC. But a Babylonian clay tablet, Plimpton 322, lists such triples more than a thousand years earlier.
And builders still use the trick: measure three along one wall and four along the other, and if the gap is five, the corner is square.
So the big square is the sum of the two small ones, on every right triangle there is.
