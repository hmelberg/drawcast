On the third of December 1992, a twenty-two-year-old engineer, Neil Papworth, sent two words: Merry Christmas.
He typed it on a computer, because no phone could write a message yet.
It reached Richard Jarvis of Vodafone, at his office Christmas party, and he had no way to reply.
The famous limit of one hundred and sixty characters is older still.
In 1985, Friedhelm Hillebrand counted the letters on postcards and telex messages, and found that was enough to say most things.
So texting began as a Christmas greeting that could not be answered.
