By 1992, less than half a second of work, for the same hour of light.
On the way down, kerosene lamps cut it to about twenty minutes by the 1870s,
and electric bulbs to under a minute by 1920.
Five hours to half a second: about forty-five thousand times less work.
Turn it around. In 1800, an hour of work bought about eleven minutes of candlelight.
By 1992, the same hour bought a bulb burning day and night for most of a year.
And price statistics missed most of it: they tracked candles and lamps, not light itself, as the economist William Nordhaus showed.
So the light your ancestors saved candle stubs for had become almost free.
