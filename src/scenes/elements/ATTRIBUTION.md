# Where the periodic table's data comes from

`elements.json` is generated by `scripts/build-elements.mjs`; do not edit it by
hand. Its three kinds of fact have three different provenances, and the split
is deliberate — nothing measured is written by hand anywhere in this repo.

## Measured values — PubChem, via `periodic-table-data`

`mass`, `config`, `electronegativity`, `radius`, `melt`, `boil`, `density`,
`category`, `state20` and `discovered` come from the npm package
[`periodic-table-data`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/periodic-table-data)
(MIT). Its table is PubChem's *Periodic Table of Elements* export — a work of
the U.S. National Library of Medicine, in the public domain.

The package is a **devDependency**: nothing at runtime depends on it. Re-run
`node scripts/build-elements.mjs` to regenerate.

Two normalisations are applied and are worth knowing about:

- **Electron configurations are re-ordered, never re-counted.** The source
  lists subshells in filling (Aufbau) order, e.g. `[Ar]4s2 3d6`. The build
  sorts by principal quantum number and then by subshell, which is the
  textbook convention exactly: `[Ar]3d⁶4s²`. Occupancies are untouched, so
  genuine exceptions survive — copper stays `[Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹` and lanthanum stays
  `[Xe]5d¹6s²`.
- **Missing measurements stay `null`.** 23 elements have no measured
  electronegativity, 25 no boiling point. They are never defaulted to zero,
  and the template never gives a `null` cell a colour.

## Structural values — derived here

`group`, `period`, `block` and `f_index` are computed from the atomic number
in the build script, not read from any source: they follow from the table's own
shape, and deriving them means the grid a template draws and the data its
drills read can never disagree. The f-block carries `group: null` — whether
lutetium and lawrencium "belong to" group 3 is a live argument in chemistry,
and a drawing should not silently take a side.

## Names — `names.json`, hand-verified

English, Norwegian (bokmål) and Latin names live in `names.json`, the one file
here written by hand, because no machine-readable source covers all three.

Every Norwegian name was checked against **two independent sources that agree
on all 118**: the wikitext of no.wikipedia's *Liste over grunnstoffer* (parsed
from the raw `{{Grunnstoff/Linje}}` rows), and Store norske leksikon
(`snl.no`), where each article's title was machine-checked to carry both the
name and the matching symbol. Norwegian keeps Latinate spellings more often
than one would guess — `Scandium`, `Technetium`, `Thorium`, `Actinium` are the
correct forms and the nativised spellings do not exist — and element 117 is
`Tenness`, not *Tennessin*.

`la` is filled in **only for the eleven elements whose symbol the English name
does not explain**: Na natrium, K kalium, Fe ferrum, Cu cuprum, Ag argentum,
Sn stannum, Sb stibium, W wolframium, Au aurum, Hg hydrargyrum, Pb plumbum.
The candidate set was derived mechanically (any symbol whose letters do not
occur in order in the English name) rather than recalled, which is why it is
exactly eleven and not "the ones we remembered".

One caveat, recorded rather than smoothed over: tungsten's **W** comes
immediately from the German *Wolfram*, not from Latin. *Wolframium* is
nonetheless the New Latin name of element 74 (it is la.wikipedia's title for
it), and it is what answers "why W?", so it is what `names: la` shows.
